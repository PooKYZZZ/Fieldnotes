# Site reliability engineering and service objectives

## Scenario

A service objective is useful only when request eligibility, failures and measurement window are explicit enough for another person to reproduce the calculation.

## Learning objectives

- Define one synthetic availability-style SLI before counting requests.
- Calculate the sample result and error budget independently from the same window.
- Explain how low volume, missing events or exclusions could mislead the decision.

## Expected observations

A second calculation using the same eligibility rules produces the same numerator, denominator and percentage. The error budget follows from the stated SLO over that same window. A small synthetic sample illustrates arithmetic only; it cannot establish production reliability or a guarantee.

## Prerequisites

Complete or study Module 22 in 03_Operations_and_Security.pdf.

## Challenge

Define one SLI for your service; calculate its result from a sample log set; propose one error-budget
policy appropriate for a lab.

## Build outcome

Write a service-level objective page, an incident classification and a small automation opportunity
assessment.

## Environment

Use a small synthetic request log or test-service metrics. Do not copy production logs or user data into the exercise.

## Lab steps

1. Define one service-level indicator in plain language, including a numerator, denominator, measurement window,
   and what counts as a valid request.
2. Create a tiny sample log with known successful, failed, and excluded requests. Label it synthetic.
3. Calculate the SLI by hand or with a short script, then check the result independently.
4. Propose a realistic lab SLO and derive the error budget for the same measurement window.
5. Describe one operational decision the budget should influence and one reason the metric could mislead.

## Hints

For an availability-style indicator:
`eligible successful requests / all eligible requests × 100`.
Choose eligibility rules before counting; exclude only documented requests such as health probes when the objective
defines that exclusion.

## Verification

- A second person can reproduce the numerator, denominator, window, and final calculation.
- The error budget is consistent with the SLO and window.
- The policy describes how the budget affects release or reliability work without pretending the lab data predicts
  production.
- Limitations such as low volume, missing telemetry, and biased exclusions are recorded.

## Troubleshooting

- **Different counts from the same log:** define duplicate, retry, probe, and malformed-record handling.
- **An apparently perfect SLI:** check whether failed or slow requests were omitted from the denominator.
- **A percentage is treated as a guarantee:** explain sample size, measurement scope, and uncertainty.

## Cleanup

Retain the formula, sample size, and sanitized fixture. Delete any copied production logs; this lab should not require them.

## Evidence to record

Include the synthetic sample and window, eligibility and exclusion rules, numerator, denominator, formula, independent calculation, resulting budget, operational policy and sample-size limitations.

## Self-check

Explain your measurement window, failure criteria and tradeoffs; identify when an SLO would be
misleading.

## Independent reflection

What would you inspect first if this stopped working tomorrow? What can you verify without asking anyone
else?
