# Site reliability engineering and service objectives

## Scenario

A service objective is useful only when request eligibility, failures and measurement window are explicit enough for another person to reproduce the calculation.

## Learning objectives

- Define one synthetic availability-style SLI before counting requests.
- Calculate the sample result and error budget independently from the same window.
- Explain how low volume, missing events or exclusions could mislead the decision.

## Expected observations
For the synthetic window of 100 eligible requests, 98 successes produce a 98% availability-style SLI. At a 99% SLO over those same 100 requests, the sample allowance is one unsuccessful request; two failures consume 2/1, or 200%, of that sample budget. These are teaching numbers for arithmetic only, not a production reliability estimate. Changing eligibility rules or the time window changes both the SLI and the budget.

## Prerequisites
Study Module 22 in 03_Operations_and_Security.pdf. Review Google SRE's [error-budget policy](https://sre.google/workbook/error-budget-policy/) and [SLO implementation guidance](https://sre.google/workbook/implementing-slos/) for how the objective, measurement window, and response policy fit together.

## Challenge
Using the sample in Hints, calculate the SLI and the 99% objective's request-based error budget by hand, then verify with an independent calculation. Explain one operational decision this tiny sample could inform and why it cannot predict future reliability.

## Build outcome

Write a service-level objective page, an incident classification and a small automation opportunity
assessment.

## Environment

Use a small synthetic request log or test-service metrics. Do not copy production logs or user data into the exercise.

## Lab steps
1. Define an availability-style SLI before counting: eligible requests, successful response rule, denominator, and a one-hour sample window. State whether retries and health checks count; for this exercise, exclude health checks and count each of the 100 synthetic application requests once.
2. Use the known synthetic sample: 98 eligible requests returned 2xx, and 2 eligible requests returned 5xx. Do not add real service logs or user data.
3. Calculate `good / eligible × 100` and compare it with a 99% SLO for the same 100-request window. Calculate the sample error allowance as `eligible × (1 - SLO)`; compare observed failed requests to that allowance.
4. Recalculate with a script or calculator independently. Confirm numerator, denominator, window, eligibility, SLI, allowed failures, and budget consumed are explicit.
5. Write a small policy: what action would you take if a representative service exceeded its budget, who would decide, and what evidence would trigger the action? Then identify why a one-hour 100-request sample is too small to support a production claim.

## Hints
Sample results:

| Measure | Calculation | Result |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Eligible requests | Given synthetic sample | 100 |
| Good requests | Given synthetic sample | 98 |
| Availability-style SLI | `98 / 100 × 100` | 98% |
| Allowed failures at a 99% SLO | `100 × (1 - 0.99)` | 1 request |
| Budget consumed | `2 observed failures / 1 allowed failure` | 200% |

For a request-based objective, the error allowance follows from `eligible events × (1 - SLO)`. The SLO and observed SLI must use the same event definition and window. In a real policy, low traffic, missing telemetry, latency objectives, exclusions, and longer rolling windows need explicit treatment.

## Verification
- A second calculation reproduces 98/100 = 98%, one allowed failure, and two failures = 200% of the sample allowance.
- The request eligibility, excluded probes/retries, window, numerator, and denominator are stated before drawing a conclusion.
- The proposed operational policy says what action follows a budget miss and who owns it, without presenting this sample as a forecast or guarantee.
- Limitations such as low volume, missing events, bias, and unrepresentative window are recorded.

## Troubleshooting
- **Different request counts:** agree on whether retries, probes, duplicate events, malformed lines, and client errors belong in the denominator before counting.
- **Budget has a fractional request:** the arithmetic can be fractional for a mathematical allowance; explain rounding/aggregation policy instead of silently changing the SLO.
- **A perfect result looks convincing:** inspect whether failures or slow requests were omitted and whether the sample window is representative.
- **The sample percentage is called “reliability”:** rename it a result for this synthetic sample and state the scope and uncertainty.

## Cleanup

Retain the formula, sample size, and sanitized fixture. Delete any copied production logs; this lab should not require them.

## Evidence to record
Include the synthetic sample/window, eligibility and exclusion rules, numerator/denominator, formula, 98% result, 99% sample allowance and budget-consumption calculation, independent check, one policy action, and limitations. Do not include production log lines or claim the sample predicts future service reliability.

## Self-check

Explain your measurement window, failure criteria and tradeoffs; identify when an SLO would be
misleading.

What would you inspect first if this stopped working tomorrow? What can you verify without asking anyone
else?
